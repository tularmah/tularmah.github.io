MEGR 3171 Mechatronics · Team project · Fall 2026

Heart-Rate Gauge

People with heart conditions, or recovering from a heart attack, are often told to keep their heart rate under a limit. My team, Mog Mob, built a desk-sized dial that reads heart rate like a speedometer and warns in plain words before it climbs too high: "All clear" up to 100 BPM, "Something's off..." up to 149, and "GET OUT NOW!" from 150 to 200.

We modeled 6 parts in Onshape with drawings and tolerances, 3D printed them in PLA, and wired a PulseSensor, servo-driven needle and OLED screen to an Arduino that filters the signal. We tested it on the whole team during a horror game, checking against hand-counted pulses with calipers, slow-motion video and a dated build log.

Compressed-Air Engine

I built a working engine from the 3/8" Square Steam Engine drawing set (original design by Roland Hege), running it on compressed air. I modeled the full assembly and every component in SolidWorks, then made all the parts myself on a manual lathe and vertical mill, working from drawings with tolerances as tight as +0.0005 in. on the shaft. After fit-up, troubleshooting and final assembly, the engine ran at up to 6,000 RPM.

Belt Buckle FEA Analysis

Designed a two-part belt buckle with a strap pulling down on both sides of the loop. I calculated the length, width and height of each part, then applied the given tensile force in SolidWorks Simulation and sized the buckle so it could support the required weight.
